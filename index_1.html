Prompt 1 · Vague

How many pallets do I need for 3,200 cartons?
AI response
  1. I can't give an exact number yet, because it depends on how many cartons fit on one pallet.
  2. pallets = ⌈ total cartons ÷ cartons per pallet ⌉ The formula is right: always round up.
  3. Illustrative examples for 3,200 cartons: 40 per pallet → 80 pallets; 64 → 50; 80 → 40; 100 → 32; 128 → 25. The arithmetic is right, but the examples are invented. Only 80 and 128 cartons per pallet match our options.
  4. It depends on the carton size, the pallet size (for example 120 × 100 cm), the maximum stack height and the maximum weight. If you send those, I can calculate the exact number.

Hidden assumption · Prompt 1

The answer assumes that all cartons are the same and every pallet is full. It gives no number we can use for the decision. A vague prompt gets a vague answer.

Prompt 2 · Detailed

You are a logistics analyst. Cartons are 30×25×20 cm and 2.4 kg. The pallet is 120×100 cm, 15 cm high, 25 kg. Maximum 8 layers. Calculate: cartons per layer (check both orientations), cartons per pallet for 5 and 8 layers, pallets for 3,200 cartons, and containers (40′ HC = 20 pallets, 20′ = 10 pallets, no stacking of pallets). Show every step and list your assumptions.
AI response
  1. Step 1 · Orientation 1 (30 cm along the 120 cm side): ⌊120 ÷ 30⌋ × ⌊100 ÷ 25⌋ = 4 × 4 = 16 cartons per layer.
  2. Step 1 · Orientation 2 (rotated 90°): ⌊120 ÷ 25⌋ × ⌊100 ÷ 30⌋ = 4 × 3 = 12. The 4.8 and 3.33 are rounded down, so space is wasted.
  3. Upper bound: pallet area 12,000 cm² ÷ carton footprint 750 cm² = 16. Orientation 1 reaches it with no waste, so it uses 16 per layer.
  4. Step 2 · Cartons per pallet: 5 layers: 16 × 5 = 80. 8 layers: 16 × 8 = 128.
  5. Step 3 · Pallets: 3,200 ÷ 80 = 40. 3,200 ÷ 128 = 25. Both divide exactly, so there is no partial pallet.
  6. Step 4 · Containers, 5 layers: 40 ÷ 20 = 2 × 40′ HC, exactly full.
  7. Step 4 · Containers, 8 layers: 20 pallets in a 40′ HC and 5 in a 20′ (10 positions, half used). That is 30 positions, 5 empty. 2 × 40′ HC also works (15 empty).
  8. Which one is cheaper cannot be decided here, because this prompt gives no freight rates.
  9. Assumption 1: cartons stay upright: the 20 cm side is vertical and the 30 × 25 cm face is on the pallet. The prompt never said this. If a carton lies on its 25 × 20 cm face, up to 24 fit per layer, but each layer is 30 cm tall. Then 8 layers make 15 + 8 × 30 = 255 cm.
  10. Assumptions 2 to 6: a simple grid with no overhang; every pallet full; “layer” means one level of cartons; container limits are only the pallet positions given; pallet height and weight are not calculated because the prompt did not ask for them.

Hidden assumption · Prompt 2

The AI assumed that the cartons stand upright, and it only did what the prompt asked. It did not calculate height or weight, so it could not see our height error in Option B.

Inspector · Question 1 of 3 · Wood packaging

Your pallet is made of wood. Where is the ISPM-15 stamp, and what must it show? Remember: no stamp, no entry. Can I read it from two opposite sides, without cutting the stretch wrap?

Team answer

The stamp is on the two short sides of the pallet, on the wooden blocks. These are two opposite sides, so you can read it from both. We keep that area free: no label and no stretch wrap on it. The stamp has four parts: the IPPC symbol, the country code PE, the producer code (who treated the wood) and HT, which means heat treated.

Inspector · Question 2 of 3 · Labels and marks

Now the marks. From the dock, I must read the shipping marks without opening anything. Which of the nine export marking items are on this pallet? Are they legible, indelible and on more than one side? What about the language and the old marks?

Team answer

The nine items are on two adjacent sides: consignee name and address, port of destination (Rotterdam), order number, gross and net weight, dimensions, port of loading (Callao), country of origin (Peru), the ship, and handling instructions. The gross weight is 332.2 kg and the dimensions are 120 × 100 × 175 cm. The labels use big letters and a material that resists water, friction and sun. We write the marks in English and check the language with the importer. We use a new pallet, so there are no old marks. The outer marks do not say the product name, to avoid theft. The net weight and the ship's name must be completed when we have the product data and the booking.

Inspector · Question 3 of 3 · Height, stability and crushing

Your pallet has 8 layers, in a column pattern, and the layers are not interlocked. Last time, three pallets from your company arrived crushed in Rotterdam, because there were too many layers on weak cartons. How do you keep this tall load stable? And how do you know the cartons at the bottom will not be crushed, also if they get wet?

Team answer

The pallet fits in the container (the 40′ HC is 269 cm high inside), so the problem is not the space. The problem is stability. We use a column pattern because it holds 16 cartons per layer. If we turn the layers 90°, it holds only 12. To hold the load as one unit we use anti-slip sheets, four corner boards, two straps, a top sheet and stretch wrap with extra turns. The bottom carton carries 7 × 2.4 = 16.8 kg. We do not know yet if the cartons are rated for 8 layers. We will ask the supplier. If they are not, we use stronger board or we use Option A with 5 layers. Corrugated board loses strength when it is wet, so we also put desiccant bags in the container.

The two biggest risks

  1. Crushing. 8 layers is the supplier's maximum. If the cartons are not rated for it, the bottom layers can be crushed, and a wet carton is weaker.
  2. Shock and vibration. A tall pallet in a column pattern can shift or lean during sea transport if the wrap and strapping are not enough.

2 changes to our pallet design

  1. Keep the ISPM-15 stamp area free of stretch wrap and labels, so it is visible on two opposite sides.
  2. Add more load holding: a third strap and more wrap turns at the base and the top, plus desiccant bags in the container. If the supplier says the cartons are not rated for 8 layers, use stronger board.

Difference found · the condition is circular

“If A is cheaper than B” only says the answer changes when it changes. A usable condition gives a number: the break-even rate. With A at 0.5% damage, B stops being cheaper when its damage rate rises above 3.40% (this value follows the sliders above). The same happens if the 20′ rate rises above US$ 2,752.

Suggested wording, using the workbook's three lines: We recommend Option B because it saves US$ 852 (US$ 5,772 against US$ 6,624). It would change if the damage rate of Option B rose above the break-even rate of 3.40%.

Break-even and air freight

Challenge 1

At what damage rate does Option B stop being cheaper than Option A?

Break-even rate of B = (total of A − freight of B) ÷ cargo value. Above this rate, B loses.

How we used the AI

AI helps us think. It does not do the thinking for us.

1The prompt that worked best

Prompt 2, the detailed one. It showed every step, checked both orientations and listed its assumptions. Its numbers matched our hand calculation: 16 cartons per layer, 80 and 128 per pallet, 40 and 25 pallets.

2One mistake or weak spot of the AI

The vague prompt gave no number we could use, and its examples were invented. The detailed prompt only answered what we asked, so it did not calculate height or weight. It also assumed that the cartons stand upright without being told.

3One change we made because of it

We checked our Stage 1 table with the AI and corrected the height of Option B from 165 cm to 175 cm.
